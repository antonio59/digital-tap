**Framing notes**
- £164.7m / 22.2m is calendar year 2023 across all PAYG media. £231.5m / 36.3m
  covers 25 Jan 2023 – 28 Feb 2024 (13 months) and is contactless card + mobile

  Do not present the two as directly comparable.
- "Average charge £6.39 per incomplete journey" is TfL's own stated average.

## Device clash (the cleanest win)

- Tapping in on a phone and out on a watch (same bank account) produces **two**
  maximum fares — the gates still open, so passengers only notice later.
- TfL refunds **£200,000+/year** for device clash; London TravelWatch calls it
  "the tip of the iceberg" and confirms **no technical fix exists** on TfL's side.
- Sources: London TravelWatch, 8 May 2024; TfL FOI-1843-2425; TfL FOI-1105-2526.
- Digital Tap eliminates this: one registered device identity, no per-device tokens.

## The market is ready

- 69% of all TfL fare-payer journeys already on contactless (Q4 2023/24, up
  from 66%); 4.2m contactless journeys/day; buses 58% contactless.
  Source: TfL Customer Service & Operational Performance Report Q4 2023/24;
  TfL FOI-0368-2526.
- 57% of UK adults used mobile wallets in 2024 (first time over half; 42% in
  2023). 18.9bn contactless payments in 2024 (~61% of card payments).
  Source: UK Finance Payment Markets Report 2025.
- ~93% of UK mobile users own a smartphone (2024). Source: Ofcom/Uswitch.
- Total TfL fares revenue 2023/24: £5,045m (FOI-3644-2425).

## Accessibility demand

- 7.1m trips/day in London involve a mobility barrier: 1.3m by disabled
  people, 4.9m carrying heavy luggage, 1.5m with under-5s, 0.7m aged 74+.
  11% of Londoners are disabled; 1.4m Freedom Pass holders (17% of the
  population). Source: TfL "Your accessible transport network".
- All 45 DLR stations are step-free — but step-free is not touch-free.
  Validators still demand a physical reach; TfL's own Access DLR trial
  acknowledges step-free "only addresses one element of accessibility".
  Source: TfL Access DLR press release.

## DLR context

- 98.9m journeys, £141.4m revenue (year ending Mar 2024) — England's busiest
  light rail; peak 122m (2016/17). Source: DfT Light Rail & Tram Statistics.
- 45 stations, ~24 miles, all unbarriered — validators only.
- Busiest stations: Bank ~30m, Canary Wharf ~18m, Woolwich Arsenal ~15m
  entries/exits (2019 TfL station data); Canary Wharf busiest DLR stop in 2025
  (6.1m). Beckton Park least busy (~279k).
- 54 new B23 trains rolling out (walk-through, +10% capacity each, +50%
  network capacity when complete; 3 wheelchair spaces, mobile charging).
  Framing: "new trains, same broken validators".

## Claims to avoid / handle with care

- "£231.5m in 2023" — wrong: it is a 13-month window. Say "in the 13 months
  to February 2024".
- Device-clash £200k is refunds *paid*, not total overcharging — present it as
  the floor ("tip of the iceberg").
- Hygiene: keep qualitative (shared high-touch surfaces); no robust
  transmission stat exists.
- Never imply TfL affiliation or endorsement.

## Suggested headline formulations

1. "TfL charged passengers £164.7 million in maximum fares for missed
   tap-outs in a single year — £231.5 million on contactless alone in 13
   months. Most of it is refundable only if you notice, and claim, in 8 weeks."
2. "The DLR has London's highest rail fare-evasion rate (4%) — on a network
   with no gates. A system that taps you in automatically removes the problem
   instead of policing it."
3. "Government is already trialling automatic check-in/check-out ticketing on
   National Rail (30,000+ journeys and counting). London is behind."
